feat: a two-input balance proof, using the machinery that already exists

For two terms the base-4 carry is binary -- C1's observation, and `carry.py` asserts it -- so
the carry polynomial is just one more packed bit polynomial: one range row, one beta
challenge, and the binaryness check that already exists proves it. No new mechanism.

Exercised end to end: a spend carrying two amounts and a carry polynomial over five range
rows proves and verifies, and a carry whose first position holds 2 is REFUSED by the proof.
That is a sound balance constraint for a two-input transaction.

WHAT THIS IS NOT: `m`-input balance. With `m` inputs the carry reaches `m` and stops being
binary, so the binaryness check says nothing about it. That case needs [25]'s unstructured
linear proof -- `z = R f + y` with its own challenge matrix, drawn after the carry
commitment and `h`, expanded from a transcript seed, with the `2^-n` lemma booked separately
from the `q^-4` of the ring challenges. It is specified in C1's and C2's eighth pass and it
is NOT implemented; the module docstring and the test say so rather than letting the
existence of the two-term test imply it.

The distinction is the difference between a sound balance proof for a two-input transaction
and one for any number of inputs, and it is worth stating in exactly those terms.
