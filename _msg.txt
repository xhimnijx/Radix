fix: B0's security estimate was stale, and k' = 1 cost 8 bits

The estimate check is pinned to the current parameters, which is the only reason this
surfaced. W = kappa + lambda + rows + 2m + 1 with rows = k + k', so reducing k' from 4 to
1 shrank the commitment width from 33 to 30. B0's dimension is W*d: 4224 before, 3840 now.
Its Core-SVP estimate fell from 2^175 to 2^167.

Neither reviewer caught the coupling and neither did I. C2 priced the tag's own collision
instance at 2^213 and the extra module equations, and concluded k' = 1 was the better
point -- all correct -- but the effect on B0's width was not in that analysis. The number
we published for B0 was wrong for several rounds.

The decision stands: 2^167 clears 128 with 39 bits to spare, and the binding relation is
still A at 2^158. But the trade is now stated at both ends -- 8 bits of B0 against 2 KB
and a fifth of the set-matrix work per tagged proof -- and it is worth re-pricing with
C2 now that both are visible.

Also: the estimator now reports named cost models, which is what C1 asked for. ADPS16
(0.292*b) and MATZOV (0.265*b) side by side, with a band of +/-15 bits from C2's measured
sensitivity of about 15 bits per half-bit of target. The band is not a point estimate and
the report says so.

The estimator itself still cannot run here: it needs SageMath, Docker Desktop is installed
but its daemon needs WSL2 or Hyper-V, neither is present, and no C++ toolchain exists for
fpylll. The named-model computation is the part of the answer that does not need it.
