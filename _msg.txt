feat: the carry proof, resolved from [25] and measured at our parameters

The user added both papers to studies/. [25] is 2020-1183.

THE ANSWER IS BETTER THAN THE PLAN. SMILE's vague reference to "an approximate shortness
proof" is the relaxed range proof on p. 5 of [25], and it needs no bit-decomposition of
the carry at all. One observation carries it:

  for f in Z^k and masking y in Z^n, over random R in {0,1}^{n x k}, the probability
  that max(R f + y mod q) >= half of max(f) is at least 1 - 2^-n.

So if R f + y is small, f was small. The prover sends z = R f + y, rejection-samples to
hide f, and proves z = R f + y with a linear argument. The verifier checks one norm
bound. That is one masked opening, not k binary proofs -- the "negligible additional
cost" SMILE refers to.

Also from p. 4: for ADDITION the carry IS binary, and the relation is a + b + f(X-2) = c
with M the (k+1)xk matrix of -2 on the diagonal and 1 below. The carry only stops being
binary when summing MANY integers, which is exactly the multi-input case.

radix_core/candidate_b/lattice/relaxed.py implements the observation, derives R from a
seed rather than transmitting it, and measures it. Five tests: with 64 columns, 128 rows
and a mask of width 64, an honest carry peaks at about 417 against a bound of 576, a
coefficient inflated 40x is caught 50 times out of 50, and an honest carry is rejected 0
times out of 50.

The first version used a UNIFORM mask, which makes z uniform and rejects every honest
proof -- 200 of 200 flagged. The mask must be bounded for the check to mean anything,
which is the same reason lattice signatures reject rather than sample. That mistake is
recorded in the module docstring.

cites the resolved technique, the addition relation, and the measurements.
