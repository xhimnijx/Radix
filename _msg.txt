feat: multi-hop discovery, so a node is joinable from a network it has not met

One hop turned "configure every peer" into "configure one". Two hops makes a node
reachable from a network it has never spoken to directly, which is what a published seed
node needs -- a newcomer configures the seed and finds the rest.

`discover_all(depth=2)` walks the peer graph breadth-first, never adding a node twice and
never adding itself, so a cycle among peers terminates: each hop only expands nodes that
were not already known. Unreachable peers are logged and skipped rather than aborting the
walk. Depth is bounded and defaults to 2; beyond that the graph is expensive for little
gain at these sizes, and the docstring says so.

Two tests: a three-node chain where C configures only A and still learns B, and a cycle
deep enough that a naive walk would not terminate.

20 p2p tests. Network hardening now has authenticated transport, multi-hop discovery and
the slow-start difficulty bootstrap; checkpoints-in-sync is the one piece left in that
item, and it is the least valuable because the measured win was 1.1x.
