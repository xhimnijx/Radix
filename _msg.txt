feat: checkpoints, with the break-even point measured rather than assumed

Chain.rebuild replays from genesis. Measured on this machine: an empty block costs about
1 ms, and a transaction adds 2.7 ms because its proof must be decoded from the wire
format. At ten transactions per 120-second block that is 27 ms per block, or 3.9 hours
to replay one year of chain -- and replay does not verify the proofs, which cost 105 ms
each.

radix_testnet/checkpoint.py snapshots the state at a tip and restores it without
replaying history: outputs, the coin pool, spent tags, the treasury, immature coinbase
outputs, and the tip block itself, because `height` is derived from the chain's length
and the anchor is the tip's hash. The 1024 decoys are NOT stored: they are derived from
a fixed seed, so storing them would be 2 MB of bytes every node can regenerate.

THE MEASUREMENT IS NOT THE ONE I EXPECTED, and it is recorded honestly. Restoring a
200-block chain takes 197 ms against 214 ms to replay it: 1.1x, not an order of
magnitude. Almost all of the restore cost is regenerating the decoy pool. Break-even is
therefore about 73 transactions skipped -- seven blocks at ten transactions each -- and on
a chain of empty blocks a checkpoint is marginally SLOWER than replaying.

Two consequences worth stating rather than discovering later. Checkpoints only pay for
chains that carry transactions, and the fixed cost could be cut by caching or serialising
the decoy pool.

The module says plainly that a checkpoint is a TRUST ANCHOR and not a consensus rule: a
node that restores from one accepts what it describes without re-deriving it, which is
why it saves work and why a bad checkpoint is unrecoverable.

Also: the first version stored only the state and not the tip block, so a restored chain
reported height 0 and could not accept the next block. The snapshot now carries the tip,
and the block list is padded to it -- absent history rather than fake transactions.
