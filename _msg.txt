research: the balance relation does not compose, and that is the open question now

I set out to assemble a full hidden-amount balance proof and found a structural gap instead of
a bug. It is worth committing because it changes what the next step is.

The binaryness check requires messages that are CONSTANT IN EACH CRT SLOT. That is what makes
`b*b = b` hold, and it is how `digits.pack_bits` builds them.

The base-4 carrying identity requires multiplication by `(X - 4)` in the COEFFICIENT domain,
where evaluating at `X = 4` recovers the integer and `(X - 4)` therefore divides the difference.

Those are different subspaces. Verified directly: multiplying a slot-constant polynomial by
`(X - 4)` leaves the slot-constant subspace -- a slot-constant 1 in slot 0 becomes
`(-4, 1, 0, 0)` in that slot. So a balance relation written over the bit planes is NOT the
identity it appears to be; it would verify and mean nothing. `balance.relations` was written,
found wanting, and REMOVED rather than shipped.

What is kept is sound and tested: `balance.build` computes the carry as the quotient of the
digit-polynomial difference by `(X - 4)` by synthetic division, refuses a spend whose amounts
do not add up, refuses a negative amount, refuses an empty input list, and checks the carry
against the input count. Four tests.

The block, stated as one question: resolving the mismatch needs either coefficient-domain
amounts whose range is proven by a quartic membership test -- which the quadratic alpha
machinery does not express -- or slot-wise carrying, which the `(X - 4)` argument does not
give. Each packing is sound alone. Which one the construction should use is a decision I
cannot make safely for the scheme, so it is recorded in docs/PAPER.md section 8 and the goal is
marked blocked on it rather than guessed at.

This is the first time in this work that a genuine design question has stopped progress. It is
also the reason to stop rather than push: a balance relation that verifies while meaning
nothing is the worst failure mode available, and it would have looked like success.
