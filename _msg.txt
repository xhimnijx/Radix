C1's eighth-pass catch, and it was a real bug. The relation at coefficient 32 is
left_32 + right_32 + f_31 - 4*f_32 = total_32 with total_32 = 0, which determines f_32 --
so the carry vector is 33 coefficients while the amount stays 32 digits. The first version
looped only over the digits and dropped the final carry, silently mis-handling any sum that
reached the top position.

The overflow case is now refused rather than carried, and that distinction matters: two
amounts near 2^64 sum past 2^64, no 32-digit total represents that, and the relation has
no exact solution. The carry absorbs intermediate carries, not a mint. My first test
expected a carry to be reported; the correct expectation was a refusal.

ALPHA BUDGET: both reviewers say two per amount, not three. The two packed bit-polynomials
need INDEPENDENT alphas -- C2's reason is decisive: folding one into an existing alpha gives
alpha(v^2 - v + b^2 - b) = 0, which a prover satisfies with v^2 - v = -(b^2 - b) != 0 and
so breaks the ring selector. The digit and carry relations are linear and need no alpha at
all; the carry's smallness is a norm check on the relaxed proof's z. The spare the old
formula kept for a two-term binary carry is not needed in this design.

TRUNCATION FIGURE SETTLED. C2 suggested 2*Phi(-12) is about 2^-101.9 per coefficient and
2^-95.9 aggregated, four bits above the paper's 2^-100. Computed exactly:
erfc(12/sqrt(2)) = 3.5530e-33 = 2^-107.8 per coefficient, and sqrt(4224)*that = 2^-101.8
aggregated. His 2^-101.9 would need Phi(-12) = 2^-102.9, a factor of thirty smaller than
the true value. The convention is now named in SECURITY-NOTES.md: 2*Phi(-12) per
coefficient, summed by the square-root bound.
