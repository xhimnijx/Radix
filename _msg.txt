fix: the peer listener ships, so the network is reachable from the product

The independent review's sharpest engineering point: p2p.py could relay, sync, discover and
authenticate, and NOTHING IN THE SHIPPED PRODUCT EVER OPENED THE PORT. serve() started the HTTP
server and stopped there, so a node could not be dialled by the peers it already knew about. The
peer layer existed only inside tests -- which is why it looked complete in the tests and was
absent from anything a person could run.

serve() now starts a p2p.Server alongside HTTP, records the bound address on the node so
discovery can advertise it, and stops the listener on shutdown. The CLI gains --p2p-port: 8643 by
default, 0 for an ephemeral port, -1 to disable.

Verified over a real socket rather than by inspection: a node started on an ephemeral port
answers a hello with its height, its cumulative work and its genesis hash, which is what a peer
dials to check it is on the same chain.

Five tests, three of which assert the wiring (the listener is constructed AND started AND its
address recorded AND stopped) because the defect was precisely an omission that no unit test of
p2p.py could catch.

One honest note on the suite: test_the_auto_miner_settles_the_mempool_by_itself failed once in a
full-file run and passed three times in isolation afterwards. It is a timing-dependent auto-miner
test under load, not a regression from this change -- serve() is not on that path. Recorded rather
than hidden, and worth a proper fix later.
