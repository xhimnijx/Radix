feat: slow start, so a fresh chain cannot be mined flat in minutes

docs/CONSENSUS.md section 3.5 flagged this as NOT DECIDED and as the parameter most
likely to be got wrong. It is decided now.

For the first BOOTSTRAP_BLOCKS the target follows a fixed schedule -- halving every
BOOTSTRAP_STEP blocks from the genesis target down to the hardest the chain allows --
rather than the measured interval. Without it, a chain that starts with an easy target is
mined flat by whoever starts first, which the 2x clamp only bounds rather than prevents.

The schedule is a hard ceiling on EASINESS, not on hardness: the measured target may be
harder than the schedule and never easier. That asymmetry is deliberate -- an early miner
who is honest and slow should not be punished for it, while an early miner who arrives
first must not be rewarded for that either.

Measured: 2^50 at height 1, halving every 100 blocks, reaching the 2^32 floor after about
1,800 blocks and ending at 4,000. Four tests: the schedule halves, it never asks for less
than the floor, a fast miner cannot outrun it, and the measured target takes over once the
window closes.

A pre-existing test had to move: `test_slow_blocks_make_the_target_larger` asserted that
slow blocks ease difficulty off, which slow start now forbids DURING the window -- that is
the feature, not a regression. The test now fast-forwards past the window and asserts the
same property where it applies. Its timestamps were also in 2033, which the future rule
rejects, so it had been passing for the wrong reason before.

16 difficulty tests, and the chain, node and reward suites still pass.
