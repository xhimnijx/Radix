feat: digit membership, the noise-aware statement, and one-hop discovery

The three things that were specified but not built.

DIGIT MEMBERSHIP (radix_core/candidate_b/lattice/digits.py, 14 tests).
Amounts as 32 base-4 digits; each digit as two bits; the bits packed as slot-constant
polynomials. C2's correction is what makes it affordable and it is now asserted rather
than believed: b(b-1) = 0 holds slotwise, a polynomial is 32 slots of 4 coefficients, so
one polynomial carries 32 bits. Sixty-four bit-constraints per amount cost TWO
polynomials and therefore two alpha challenges. constraint_count(4) is 9, not 256, and a
test fails if that ever regresses. It is not a one-hot over {0,1,2,3}: C1 was right that
a four-element selector is a different statement from a one-out-of-1024 selector.

THE NOISE-AWARE STATEMENT (noisy.py, 7 tests). C1's finding that the stealth key is
P = A*p + e1, an MLWE sample, so it is not a SMILE key at all. The obstruction was never
the norm -- I spent a round arguing about whether p was short enough when the real
problem was the noise. The fix implemented here is A_noise = [A | I_k] with witness (p, e1)
and tag rows [A' | 0], so the tag stays a function of the secret alone. The test that
matters is negative: the exact relation A*p = pk REJECTS a legitimately-produced noisy
key, which is why the statement had to change rather than the parameters.

ONE-HOP DISCOVERY (3 tests). A node serves the peers it knows; `discover` asks one peer
and remembers what it hears. One hop, no gossip, no self-announcement, and the docstring
says so -- a node nobody introduces still has to be configured by hand. It turns
"configure every peer" into "configure one", which is the difference between a private
testnet and something another operator can join.

These are the primitives, not the wiring: the digit proof is not yet in the statement, and
the noisy relation is linear algebra rather than a proof. Both are stated in their module
docstrings rather than implied by their presence.
