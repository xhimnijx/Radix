feat: m-input carry boundedness, proven by decomposition rather than approximation

For m inputs the base-4 carry reaches m and is no longer binary, so the alpha-slot binaryness
check says nothing about it. C1's route was [25]'s relaxed proof. This takes a different
sound route with the machinery that already exists: bit-decompose the carry.

  Keys.carry_rows   committed messages that are NOT binary, so they get no beta and no
                    range term; their boundedness comes from a LinearRelation
  carry - sum 2^i b_i = 0

The carry for m inputs lies in [0, m], so ceil(log2(m+1)) bits per position suffice, and
those bits are ordinary range rows proven binary by the existing check. The carry is
therefore bounded because it IS the sum of proven bits, not because a norm bound was hoped
for. At m = 16 that is five bit planes plus one carry row -- cheaper than the relaxed proof
at this size and built from checks that are already tested.

Bit planes are packed one polynomial per plane. That ordering is load-bearing: packing
position-major would scatter each position's bits across different polynomials and the
relation could not be written as a fixed weight per row.

Three tests: a four-input carry decomposes and verifies; a carry that does not match its
bits is REFUSED; and a bit plane with a 2 in it is refused by the range check as usual.

Two errors of mine: f_extra was computed before f_range existed (UnboundLocalError), and the
first version of this test file packed position-major and built the relation twice while
editing. Both were caught by running it.
