fix: a chain adopted from a peer is now verified, not trusted

The most serious gap I have found in the network layer, and it was not in the
cryptography. `_apply` decoded a peer's transactions and checked their tags, but never
verified a proof. A node adopting a chain from a stranger therefore accepted every spend
in it on no cryptographic evidence -- the proof of work, the block links and the tags were
checked, and the signatures were not. That is replication of whatever you are told, not
validation, and on a mainnet it means any peer can hand you a chain of invalid spends.

`_apply` gains verify_transactions, `append` and rebuild forward it, and rebuild DEFAULTS
to True so a new sync path cannot reintroduce the gap by forgetting a flag. Locally mined
blocks skip it, because they were validated at submission and re-checking them is waste.
The measured cost of the safe default is about 105 ms per transaction with the Rust
verifier, which is the price of not trusting a stranger.

Node.sync_from now passes verify_transactions=True explicitly, with the reason in a
comment: this chain came from a stranger.

Three test-setup faults of mine on the way, each caught by running: the test chain's
genesis was an arbitrary key rather than the protocol allocation, so every block looked
like a fork; the mined timestamps were set in 2027 and the future rule rejected them; and
one test was written in a way that could not distinguish the two paths it claimed to
compare.
