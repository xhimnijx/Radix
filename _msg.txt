feat: mempool policy and fees paid to the miner

A fee was a number the sender typed. Nothing rejected a zero fee, nothing bounded the
mempool, and the miner received none of it -- a chain where a free transaction is
indistinguishable from a paid one and a block producer is paid only by the emission.

Three rules, written as pure functions so they can be tested without producing a proof
(a real tagged spend costs about a minute, which would make fee ordering untestable):

  MIN_FEE_ATOMS = 1, because a free transaction is a denial of service with extra steps
  MEMPOOL_MAX = 64, with eviction by FEE RATE rather than absolute fee, so a large cheap
  transaction cannot displace a small expensive one
  block_reward(height, fees) credits fees entirely to the miner, with the treasury slice
  taken from the emission only

The last point is a policy choice worth stating: splitting fees would make a miner
indifferent between including a transaction and leaving it.

_apply now applies transactions BEFORE building the coinbase, because the block's fees
must be summed before the miner's output is created. The coinbase value is
emission-share + fees; the treasury still takes its percentage of the emission.

10 tests: zero fee refused, below-minimum refused, duplicate refused, fee rate is
atoms-per-byte, full pool evicts the cheapest, a worse rate is refused when full, eviction
ranks by rate and not by absolute fee, fees go to the miner and not the treasury, an
empty-fee block pays exactly the emission, and a negative fee cannot reduce the reward.

54 tests across the four affected suites still pass.
